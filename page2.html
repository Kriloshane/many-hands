When sweet dew falls from heaven, the stars come tinkling down, beating upon rooftops, upon leaves, upon car windows. The world grows misty; cats and dogs curl up in warm corners at home; squirrels and foxes dart among leaves, large and small. Car windows mist over; wheels roll, scattering sheets of spray. Fur and bark slowly blur into indistinctness— then—alas! The wheels roll over, and the little creatures lie flattened on the ground. People take up shovels and shovel and shovel, lifting the flattened little bodies and burying them in the earth, hoping they may forget the dreadful blare of horns and the dark, looming tyres, and rest in peace in heaven. So I appeal: Build fences along motorways to keep small animals from straying onto the road. On ordinary roads, drive more slowly, and do all we can to prevent roadkill.
